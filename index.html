01 · Project overview

Introduction and Need for the Project

Nutrition supports growth, energy, immunity and the prevention of disease. Nutritional awareness helps people make informed food choices, maintain food hygiene and build healthy eating habits.

About the project

This Community Service Project focuses on nutritional awareness and its impact on community health and well-being in Balaji Nagar, Nellore, Andhra Pradesh.

It explores the importance of essential nutrients, balanced food choices, safe water and food hygiene. It also highlights affordable meals made with locally available foods such as rice, millets, pulses, vegetables, fruits, milk and eggs.

Why this project is needed

  • Some families may need practical guidance to combine different food groups.
  • Irregular meals and frequent sugary drinks or packaged snacks can affect health.
  • Children, adolescents, pregnant women and older adults may have different nutritional needs.
  • Safe water, food storage and handwashing help reduce food-related illness.

03 · Learn the basics

Nutrients, Food Groups and Balanced Diet

Different nutrients perform different roles. Eating a variety of foods helps provide what the body needs.

CarbohydratesMain source of energy. Sources: rice, wheat, ragi, millets and potato.
ProteinsSupport growth and tissue repair. Sources: pulses, beans, chickpeas, eggs, milk and fish.
FatsProvide energy and support cell function. Sources: nuts, seeds, oils and fish.
VitaminsSupport immunity, vision, skin and body processes. Sources: fruits and vegetables.
MineralsSupport bones, blood and nerve function. Sources: leafy greens, pulses, dairy, nuts and seeds.
FibreSupports digestion and bowel health. Sources: whole grains, vegetables, fruits and pulses.
WaterRegulates temperature and supports body functions. Choose safe drinking water.
04 · Everyday food choices

What Is a Balanced Diet?

A balanced diet includes a variety of foods from different food groups in suitable amounts.

Build a balanced plate

Vegetables & fruit
Include a variety of colours and seasonal choices.
Cereals & millets
Rice, wheat, ragi and other millets provide energy.
Protein foods
Dal, beans, eggs, fish or another suitable option.
Milk & healthy fats
Curd, milk or alternatives; use oils in moderation.

Healthy habits to remember

  • Choose affordable, local and seasonal foods.
  • Eat regular meals and include a variety of food groups.
  • Wash fruits and vegetables before use.
  • Drink safe water and practise proper food hygiene.
  • Use salt and added sugar in moderation.
  • Enjoy meals mindfully and avoid food waste.
05 · Community participation

Survey, Activities and Implementation Plan

A simple voluntary survey can help understand existing awareness and identify topics that may be useful for the community.

Sample survey questions

  • Do you usually eat breakfast?
  • Can you name three different food groups?
  • How often do you include vegetables or fruits in your meals?
  • Do you include pulses, beans, eggs, milk or another protein source?
  • Do you check food expiry dates and storage instructions?
  • Do you wash your hands before eating?
  • Would you attend a short nutrition awareness session?

Participation note: Participation should be voluntary. Explain the purpose, avoid collecting names unless essential, and present results as group totals. A questionnaire should not be used to diagnose nutritional deficiencies.

06 · Field work

Activity Log and Weekly Reports

The weekly activities covered nutrition basics, balanced diets, life-stage nutrition, food hygiene and community awareness.

Week 1 · Nutrition awareness basics

Explored what nutrition means, major nutrients, balanced diets, safe drinking water, fruits and vegetables, and healthy food habits.

Week 2 · Malnutrition awareness

Discussed forms of malnutrition, effects of undernutrition, micronutrient deficiencies, iron, anaemia prevention and nutrition during childhood and adolescence.

Week 3 · Nutrition across life stages

Covered pregnancy, breastfeeding, complementary feeding, safe meals for children, community health services and nutrition for older adults.

Week 4 · Household visits and balanced diet

Revisited balanced diet concepts and discussed the importance of fruits and healthy food choices during community interactions.

Week 5 · Food hygiene and safety

Discussed food hygiene, safe food storage, contamination, handwashing and ways to reduce food waste.

Week 6 · Community awareness and feedback

Focused on nutrition awareness through discussions, posters and simple demonstrations, with community participation and feedback.

08 · Final reflection

Key Learnings and Conclusion

  • Nutrition awareness helps people understand the connection between food and health.
  • Students can support awareness through discussions, posters and simple demonstrations.
  • Community participation makes health messages more practical and effective.
  • Feedback can help assess understanding and improve future awareness programmes.

The activities helped us understand how informed food choices, balanced nutrition, food hygiene and community participation contribute to better health and well-being. Sharing reliable nutrition information can encourage healthier habits among families.
